You are the analyst. Locus (installed per `setup`, so `locus` is on your PATH) supplies mechanical facts and a grounded-analysis call; every judgment — what to look at, what to ask, what the findings mean — is yours. The only order is data dependency, and going back mid-investigation to load more or ask again is normal.

Mechanical facts are free SQL over the flat columns; meaning costs a model call — the operator's money on Gemini, real minutes locally. A known target (one visitor's session, a few slices, a specific moment) reads directly. A corpus-wide question is the same two moves at scale: aggregates whittle the corpus down to the sessions worth reading, and each analysis is earned by the aggregates rather than fanned out on speculation. Earlier analyses are free evidence too: each one keeps its question and answer in `analyses/` beside the db, so before paying for a new one, check whether an old answer already covers or narrows the question.

## Ground truth — read it, never restate it

- Five tables: `events`, one row per recorded event with every fact flat on the row; `slices`, one row per recording — its span, its status, and the facts constant across it; `sessions`, one row per session under the operator's definition, with every event stamped by the session that held it; `loaded_chunks`, one row per chunk object fetched; `chunk_errors`, one row per error record the recorder shipped. What each column holds is written in the schema's own comments, so read the live schema — `sqlite3 data/events.db '.schema'` — before querying, never a remembered layout; the event kinds a corpus actually holds are `SELECT DISTINCT type_str FROM events`. Query named columns and named kinds — never bare rrweb numerics out of `raw_json`. The generated constants (`evidence/distill/rrweb_constants.js`, `evidence/src/locus/evidence/rrweb_constants.py`) are the numeric↔name truth if you need it.
- When a question uses behavior vocabulary — session, engaged, bounce — those words are the operator's, defined in `config/definitions.toml` and applied in `sessions`; select by them, not by a boundary of your own. The quantify skill covers counting in that vocabulary — the planes and their biases; here the table is how a session is found and whittled to.

## What this data can and cannot testify

- **A slice is a replay unit, not a session.** A page context opens a slice when it starts and cuts a new one at each periodic checkout, so a session spans many slices, and a slice never spans two page contexts. The boundary is the operator's definition applied to the visitor's acts; a session's slices are `SELECT DISTINCT slice_id FROM events WHERE session_id = ?`, and that set is what `locus analyze` and `locus browse open` take.
- **Absence of events is not absence of behavior.** Masked input never existed downstream. Unreplayable slices are kept out of replay and analysis and counted (`slices` carries the discards and their reasons; their events stay queryable). `chunk_errors` carries the recorder's own error log from the device — the records naming a drop are capture it knew it lost. Check all three before reasoning from silence.
- **`url` is attested, `page_url` is stamped.** `url` means the event itself carried it; `page_url` is every event stamped with the page it happened on. Per-page anything uses `page_url`.
- **The device columns are testimony.** `device`/`os`/`browser`, `language`, `time_zone`, and `screen_width`/`screen_height` come from the recording browser; no event carries them and nothing derives them. `screen` is the display, not the viewport that rrweb's Meta records — a gap between the two means a windowed browser. Every timestamp is UTC, so `time_zone` is the only way to know what hour it was where the visitor sat.
- **Structure is queryable; content is not.** Columns answer what happened, where, when, in what order, and how often — every question that is a function of the acts. What a visitor experienced — what rendered, what they saw, what a thing meant — is in the DOM and the pixels, and comes only from the model reading the session (the event stream and the screenshots). A column pattern that seems to answer intent or experience has not; spend the model call. The split runs the other way too: a count over the acts is free SQL, whether or not a model call led you to the pattern — the quantify skill covers the source particulars.
- **Raw counts mislead as size or importance proxies.** A handful of large mutations outweighs sustained interaction; duration and event mix say more than event count, and the bare `locus analyze` invocation prices the composed evidence exactly when cost is the question. `Input` events are not all typing: scripts set values too, and a tracking pixel writing its hidden form fields can dwarf a visitor's real keystrokes — the recorder flags each one (`userTriggered` in `extra`), so read the flag before trusting the count.
- **A replay plays wall-clock, compressed.** The player's timeline is recorded time, not time-on-task, and rrweb-player fast-forwards inactivity by default — so a long session plays through fast, and the skipped stretch is the skip, not lost recording. A large gap usually means the visitor stepped away; whether they left the tab or sat idle on it is read from the `PageHidden`/`PageVisible` events spanning the gap, never inferred from duration.

## The db holds only what you loaded, and only until the horizon

`locus ls` to see what the store holds, `locus load` to load it into the db, `locus status` to account for what loaded. The db is the store as of the last load, and loaded is not complete: chunks arrive late by design, so a window loaded earlier has changed since. Load it again before counting over it, or before claiming nothing happened in it. The deployment's retention horizon bounds the other end: a load drops the recordings past it and never fetches one, in the db exactly as in the bucket, so the corpus reaches back that far and no further. A question aimed further back has no evidence to answer it — which is what to say.

## Querying, looking, and the model call

Facts come from aggregate SQL over the flat columns — keep raw event content, `md`, and `diff` out of your context, since the model call is where content gets read. To see a session yourself, `locus browse open <slice set>` (or an analysis directory) materializes the replay material, composes the default page, and opens it in a window whose id comes back in the reply — re-open that window at a citation via `#t=<epoch-ms>`, and `locus browse screenshot <window>` captures it, so you look before handing off. For a page of your own (side-by-side, whatever the question needs) the composition contract is the header of `data/pages/locus-replay.js`.

For the semantic read:

```sh
locus analyze <slice ...> "<question>"   # prices the model-payload, spends nothing
```

The analysis runs on the deployment's default model, settled at setup; `--model` runs one invocation on any other declared model instead — either backend, priced and run on that model's card. Changing the default itself is a deployment change, per the change-deployment skill.

Locally that model is a resident MLX server you boot and tear down — read `analysis/README.md` § The local server (mlx) before touching its lifecycle. An analysis that fits the card's context can still run the machine out of memory, and that kills the server rather than failing the run: it means this window, on this card, on this compute, does not fit. Nothing predicts it — memory is not a function of token counts, not linear, not in any config, and no run's footprint forecasts another's — so the only instrument is the cut after the fact: drop slices or widen the screenshot interval for this window, lower the card's headroom for every window, and if it keeps happening at the sizes the operator's questions need, tell them plainly that this model does not work on this machine.

The question is entirely yours, in your words: put your actual hypothesis or comparison in it; a focused question outperforms "analyze this". Name a moment by its recording offset, or by a full UTC instant ("11:10:03 UTC"); a bare `16:07` is the offset form the model cites in, and it reads it as one.

The slice set is the evidence window: whole slices, any subset you choose. Dropping middle slices is normal whittling, and the evidence states the gap mechanically. The window may span visitors — a comparative question over several visitors' recordings is one analysis — and slices overlapping in time (a second tab, concurrent visitors) compose too. Every slice is labeled (S1, S2, …) on one shared window clock, the label abbreviating the slice id within the window, so concurrent recordings read as concurrent: their offsets genuinely overlap. A multi-slice answer cites with the slice label (`[S2 04:31.220]`), and the analysis's `window.json` carries the slice table mapping each label to its visitor, slice, and interval. One invocation is one answer, so separate deliverables are separate invocations — independent processes that run concurrently when you have several; admission is the backend's own job.

An invocation prices the composed model-payload against the model's context — exact tokens, context percentage, per-slice contributions, and on a priced model the input dollars of what is actually sent up front — and stops. Sending is a second, deliberate step. A model-payload that out-prices the context is refused with those numbers; you narrow it: drop slices, widen `--screenshot-interval`, or, when a single slice alone out-prices the context, take one of the route-boundary piece addresses (`<slice>#2`) the price report offers, so the cut lands on a page boundary. Activity-screenshots lie at a fixed interval kept near user activity, so their count tracks how continuously active a recording is, not how long it runs. Any moment, included among them or not, the model can pull at full detail mid-call — so widening the interval thins what is pushed up front, never what the model can reach. A run emits one analysis directory, self-placed in `analyses/` beside the db and named by when it ran and what it analyzed; its final response is the answer. Site context — the operator's business frame, banked during setup at `data/context/<snippet>.md` — resolves automatically from the slice set's snippet; a missing file fails loud.

The audit trail needs no model: a citation resolves by lookup in the analysis's `window.json` — the label (or the lone slice) names the recorder slice, and `window_start_ts` + the cited offset is the absolute moment a replay page's `#t=` fragment takes — so a doubted claim resolves by opening that moment in a replay page and walking the analysis's persisted transcripts and screenshots yourself.

## The operator hears it in their words

Findings come out per analysis; the deliverable rarely is. The operator gets the finding in their own vocabulary, with the moments worth their eyes — a page when looking is the point (the html-communication skill). Where the evidence ran out, say so: "the recording cannot answer this" is a finding.

Quantities belong in a finding as much as meaning does — report them. One question often takes this skill and quantify together; quantify covers the particulars of querying the different sources.
